### G. Narrative
A 45-year-old female received amoxicillin for a suspected bacterial infection. On approximately Day 3 of treatment, she developed generalized skin rash and itching. Amoxicillin was discontinued and the symptoms subsequently improved. Rechallenge was not performed. The available information supports a suspected association between amoxicillin exposure and the reported skin reaction; however, complete dose, route, exact dates, concomitant therapy, medical history, allergy history and clinical examination details should be obtained before final case assessment.

### H. Follow-up questions
1. Exact date/time amoxicillin was started?
2. Exact dose, route and frequency?
3. Exact date/time rash began?
4. Was the rash diagnosed by a healthcare professional?
5. Any mucosal involvement, facial swelling, breathing difficulty or systemic symptoms?
6. Any previous penicillin/amoxicillin allergy?
7. All concomitant medicines and recent vaccines?
8. What treatment was given for the reaction?
9. Exact stop date and outcome date?
10. Was hospitalization or emergency treatment required?
11. Was the drug restarted?
12. Product/lot information if available?

### I. Quality checks
- Four minimum elements for a valid ICSR should be confirmed in real processing: identifiable patient, identifiable reporter, suspect medicinal product and adverse event.
- Search for possible duplicates using patient/event/product/reporter and other available identifiers.
- Do not submit a simulated case as a real regulatory ICSR.

## Regulatory alignment
PvPI publishes an ADR reporting form for healthcare professionals. EMA GVP Module VI covers collection, management and submission of suspected ADR reports and includes duplicate-management guidance. ICH E2B(R3) provides the international framework for electronic ICSR data elements.
