# Real-World Pharmacovigilance & Regulatory Evidence — 2026

This file separates **real regulatory information** from the educational cases in Tasks 1–5.

## 1. India — PvPI
The Pharmacovigilance Programme of India provides ADR reporting resources and a suspected ADR reporting form for healthcare professionals.

Official source:
https://www.ipc.gov.in/PvPI/adr.html

ADR forms:
https://www.ipc.gov.in/mandates/pvpi/adr-reporting/8-category-en/865-adr-reporting-forms.html

## 2. India — CDSCO
CDSCO publishes a Pharmacovigilance Guidance Document for Marketing Authorization Holders of Pharmaceutical Products. Version 2.0 is listed by CDSCO/IPC as the current guidance document.

Official source:
https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Post-Marketing-DSM/

Version 2.0 notice:
https://www.ipc.gov.in/news-highlights/1322-pharmacovigilance-guidance-document-for-marketing-authorization-holders-of-pharmaceutical-products-version-2-0.html

## 3. WHO-UMC
WHO-UMC provides the internationally used causality assessment framework with categories such as Certain, Probable/Likely, Possible, Unlikely, Conditional/Unclassified and Unassessable/Unclassifiable.

Source:
https://who-umc.org/media/u4gjgxvv/who-umc-causality-assessment_new-logo.pdf

## 4. EMA GVP
EMA GVP Module VI covers collection, management and submission of suspected adverse reaction reports. EMA also maintains specific guidance on duplicate management and masking personal data in ICSRs.

Source:
https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/post-authorisation/pharmacovigilance-post-authorisation/good-pharmacovigilance-practices-gvp

## 5. FDA — current adverse-event surveillance
FDA's Adverse Event Monitoring System (AEMS) public dashboard was introduced in 2026 to provide public access to human adverse-event information.

Source:
https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fda-adverse-event-monitoring-system-aems/fda-adverse-event-monitoring-system-aems-public-dashboard

FDA also publishes current safety-signal information:
https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fda-adverse-event-monitoring-system-aems/new-safety-information-or-potential-signals-serious-risks-identified-fda-adverse-event-monitoring

## 6. Practical learning point
Public spontaneous-report databases are useful for signal detection and hypothesis generation. A report in a database does **not automatically establish that a medicine caused an event**. Individual case causality requires clinical assessment and appropriate context.

## 7. Important portfolio rule
Never copy real patient identifiers into a public GitHub repository. Use fictional/coded patient information and keep the portfolio clearly educational.
