# Task 4 — Severity, Seriousness & Expectedness Classification

## Objective
Classify the three assignment cases using pharmacovigilance concepts. **Severity/intensity and seriousness are not synonyms.**

## Case matrix

| Case | Reaction | Severity/intensity | Seriousness | Expectedness | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Amoxicillin — skin rash/itching | Mild–moderate, provisional | Non-serious on supplied facts | Must be checked against current product information | No hospitalization/life-threatening outcome supplied |
| 2 | Ibuprofen — GI bleeding requiring hospitalization | Severe | **Serious — hospitalization criterion** | Must be checked against applicable product information | Hospitalization makes it serious |
| 3 | Metformin — lactic acidosis | Severe | **Serious** if life-threatening/hospitalization/other seriousness criterion is present | Must be checked against applicable product information | Requires clinical confirmation and outcome |

## Case 1
**Amoxicillin → skin rash/itching**

- Severity: provisional mild–moderate.
- Seriousness: non-serious based on supplied facts.
- Expectedness: do not infer from severity; compare the event with the current applicable product information.
- Follow-up: obtain exact diagnosis, treatment and outcome.

## Case 2
**Ibuprofen → gastrointestinal bleeding requiring hospitalization**

- Severity: severe.
- Seriousness: serious because hospitalization is an accepted seriousness criterion.
- Expectedness: requires comparison with the applicable current reference safety information.
- Key point: the hospitalization criterion determines seriousness even though “seriousness” and “severity” are different concepts.

## Case 3
**Metformin → lactic acidosis**

- Severity: severe.
- Seriousness: likely serious if the clinical record documents life-threatening status, hospitalization or another applicable seriousness criterion.
- Expectedness: must be assessed against the current product information.
- Follow-up: obtain lactate, pH/acid-base data, renal function, clinical outcome, hospitalization status, dose, renal/hepatic history and alternative causes.

## Regulatory definition note
FDA describes serious adverse-event outcomes including death, life-threatening events, inpatient hospitalization/prolongation, persistent or significant disability/incapacity and congenital anomaly/birth defect. International/regional definitions can vary by reporting framework, so the applicable local/company procedure must be followed.
