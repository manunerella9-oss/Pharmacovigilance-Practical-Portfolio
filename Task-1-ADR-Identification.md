# Task 1 — ADR Identification Basics

## Objective
Identify a suspected adverse drug reaction from a clinical scenario by reviewing the medicine, indication, timeline, clinical event, action taken and outcome.

## Case A — Amoxicillin-associated skin reaction
**Case type:** Educational simulation

| Field | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Patient | 45-year-old female |
| Indication | Suspected bacterial infection |
| Suspect medicine | Amoxicillin |
| Reaction | Generalized skin rash with itching |
| Temporal relationship | Reaction began after treatment initiation |
| Dechallenge | Symptoms improved after discontinuation |
| Rechallenge | Not performed |
| Outcome | Recovering/recovered |
| Suspected ADR | Skin rash/pruritus associated with amoxicillin |

### Case assessment
The event is a **suspected ADR**, not a confirmed causal relationship. The temporal sequence and improvement after withdrawal support a drug-related association, but alternative causes should still be considered.

### What a Drug Safety Associate should check
- Exact start/stop dates and dose.
- Description of the rash, distribution and severity.
- Other medicines, infections and allergies.
- Whether medical treatment was required.
- Whether the patient was hospitalized.
- Outcome and date of recovery.
- Whether the reporter considers the drug suspect.
- Whether the reaction is already documented in the applicable product information.

### Minimum case quality principle
Do not invent missing clinical facts. Record them as **unknown/not reported** and request follow-up.

### Conclusion
**Suspected ADR:** Amoxicillin-associated skin rash/pruritus.

**Causality:** Provisional Probable/Likely, subject to adequate clinical information and assessment of alternative causes.

**Seriousness:** Not serious based on the information supplied; reassess if hospitalization, life-threatening condition or another seriousness criterion is subsequently reported.

## Evidence base
PvPI provides a suspected ADR reporting form for healthcare professionals, supporting structured capture of patient, reaction, suspected medicine and reporter information.
